The 7-Point Video Brief: How to Get the Edit You Imagined the First Time

21 November 2026 · 6 min read · By Sandmotion Video Labs

In this guide

  • Most bad edits come from unclear briefs.
  • Seven short answers give an editor what they need.
  • Timestamped feedback beats general feelings.

You send the footage, wait a few days, and the first draft is nothing like what you pictured. That is rarely the editor's fault alone. Without a brief, they are guessing, and a guess rarely matches the picture in your head.

The 7 points every brief needs

  1. Audience. Describe the viewer in one sentence. It guides pacing, music and how much to explain.
  2. One goal. Subscribers, enquiries, sales or trust. Pick one.
  3. Platform and format. YouTube, Reels or website? 16:9, 9:16 or both? Target length?
  4. References. Two or three videos you like, and what you like about each.
  5. Look and feel. Three words such as "clean, warm, confident".
  6. Brand and must-haves. Logos, fonts, colors, music licences, words that must appear or never appear.
  7. Delivery and feedback. Formats, deadline, revision rounds and how you will send notes.

Feedback that gets results

Instead of: "Make it punchier."
Try: "0:42 to 0:50 feels slow. Cut the pause and tighten to two sentences."

Specific, timestamped notes let the editor fix exactly what you see. Gather your comments into one message per round.

4 common briefing mistakes

  • Saying "make it cinematic" with no example.
  • Changing the goal halfway through.
  • Sending references with no explanation of what to copy.
  • Giving feedback from three people who disagree.
