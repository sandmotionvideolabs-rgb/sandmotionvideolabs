The short answer

Video is priced by what goes into it, not by minutes alone. Published 2026 guides put corporate video at roughly $1,000 to $10,000 per finished minute, with simple talking-head work at the low end and animation or heavy production at the top. For whole projects, one guide lists:

Social media content (Reels, Shorts)$1,500 to $5,000Length, number of clips, captions, effects
Corporate and explainer videos$4,500 to $20,000Script, style, animation, voiceover

Other sources give different numbers. A Clutch directory page puts per-minute pricing at roughly $1,000 to $5,000, a 2026 corporate video guide quotes whole projects from $5,000 to $200,000, and a European production company lists prices from about €1,100 for basic setups to over €6,000 for premium campaigns. Treat every number as a rough map, not a price tag. Your real price depends on your idea.

What about India and international clients?

Indian studios usually quote per video, per reel or per project, not per minute. One Pune agency, for example, publishes about ₹40,000 for a one-minute product video and about ₹10,000 for reels. That is one company's rate card, not a rule, but it shows how differently prices are presented in India.

If you are an international client, remember that a remote team edits, animates and delivers everything online. You are paying for creative work, not a studio in your city. The smart move is to ask a local agency and a remote team to quote the same brief, then compare what each includes.

Why the same video can cost 10 times more

When two quotes for "a 60-second video" look nothing alike, check these seven things:

  1. Style. A simple edited talking-head video costs far less than animation built from scratch.
  2. Length and versions. One 5-minute video plus ten short clips is more work than one video alone.
  3. Footage. Editing footage you already have costs less than planning, shooting and directing new footage.
  4. Motion graphics. Animated titles, logos and effects add creative hours.
  5. Sound. Licensed music, voiceover and sound design are extra.
  6. Revisions. Two agreed rounds cost less than unlimited changes.
  7. Speed. Rush deadlines cost more.
Worth knowing: a 30-second commercial can cost more per minute than a 5-minute training video. Budget by the scope of the job, not by the clock.

What has changed in 2026

Video is now standard. Wyzowl's 2026 research reports that around 91% of businesses use video as a marketing tool. The question is no longer "should we?" but "how do we make enough without overspending?"

Teams want more videos, not bigger budgets. Wistia's 2026 report says companies are making more videos while fewer plan to raise spending, and almost half are keeping budgets flat. That is why monthly plans and repurposing one video into many clips are growing: they give predictable output at a predictable cost.

AI helps, but it does not replace direction. Wistia finds AI is used most in planning, scripting and ideas, with captions the top generative use. Someone still decides what the video should say and how it should feel.

Demand is spreading across India. Reports point to creative clusters in cities like Jaipur, Lucknow and Kochi generating demand for editors, and to rising video demand in fintech, healthcare, wellness, edtech and parenting businesses. More Indian companies are now video buyers, not just creators.

How to set your budget, whoever you are

Tap your type to see where to start:

YouTubers and creators

Your biggest cost is time. Start with regular editing of videos you already shoot, plus Shorts and Reels cut from your long videos. A monthly arrangement usually beats paying per video, because the editor learns your style.

Small businesses

Make one strong core video, such as an intro, an offer or a product story, then cut it into short clips for social media. One good video used many times beats five rushed ones.

Growing brands and personal brands

You need consistency more than one big hit. A monthly creative partner keeps your look, tone and rhythm steady, so your content feels like a series.

Agencies and enterprises

You need clear scoping, approval stages and reliable delivery. Larger or ongoing projects are best planned in a short call so deliverables, versions, timelines and approvals are agreed before work starts.

7 ways to get more video for your money

  1. One goal per video. Sales, subscribers or trust, not all three.
  2. Batch your work. Several videos in one go is easier to plan.
  3. Repurpose. Turn one long video into clips, quotes and teasers.
  4. Send a clear brief. Unclear briefs cause extra revisions. Grab the free template below.
  5. Agree revision rounds upfront. Two clear rounds beat endless small changes.
  6. Gather assets early. Logos, fonts, footage and music in one folder.
  7. Start with a pilot. Test one short video before committing to a series.

Red flags in a video quote

  • A price with no description of what is included.
  • No mention of revision rounds.
  • No clarity on who owns the final video and files.
  • Pressure to pay everything upfront for a large project.
  • Promises of "guaranteed viral" results. Nobody can promise that.
